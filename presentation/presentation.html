Introduction & Domain Background

The Strategic Role of Institutional Alumni Networks in Modern Higher Engineering Education
Domain Overview

What is an Alumni Network?

•

An institutional community infrastructure bridging a university's active students, graduated alumni, and academic departments into an enduring symbiotic ecosystem.

  • Human Capital: Senior graduates working in global tech giants mentor emerging student talent.
  • Institutional Giving: Alumni contribute directly to academic laboratories, GPU compute, and scholarships.

Student Career Acceleration

•

Alumni engagement directly impacts college placement outcomes and industry-readiness:

  • 1-on-1 Mentorship: Domain advice for AI/ML, distributed systems, and product engineering.
  • Verified Job Referrals: Direct internal referral pipelines that bypass crowded third-party job boards.
  • Curriculum Insights: Real-world feedback aligning academic syllabi with market needs.

Academic Domain Demands

•

Higher education regulatory frameworks (e.g., NAAC & NBA accreditation) mandate structured alumni relations:

  • Data Accuracy: Official records must verify genuine alumni credentials.
  • Measurable Engagement: Audit trails of mentorship sessions and departmental endowment funds.
  • Centralization: Transitioning from ad-hoc social groups to verified portals.
Key Insight: An engineering college's strength is defined by the vitality of its alumni network. A verified, interactive digital platform converts informal relationships into measurable academic and career success.

Problem Statement & Real-World Inefficiencies

Critical Breakdown of Existing Alumni Management and Student Engagement Practices
Problem Analysis

1. Data Decay & Identity Impersonation

  • Rapid Contact Rot: Static graduation records stored in spreadsheets become obsolete within 12 months as alumni switch employers and cities.
  • Unverified Registrations: Open public portals have zero institutional verification, allowing unauthorized users or impostors to claim fake alumni credentials.
  • No Institutional Verification: No linkage between registration forms and official college graduation records.

2. Fragmented & Unresponsive Communication

  • Cold Messaging Friction: Students send cold InMails on LinkedIn or informal messages on social media with sub-5% response rates.
  • Scattered Channels: Conversations spread across disparate WhatsApp, Telegram, and personal email threads with zero central visibility.
  • Absence of Scheduling: No mutual agreement on date, time, or agenda leads to missed meetings and wasted time.

3. Opaque Job Referrals

  • Lost Referral Opportunities: Alumni willing to refer students to top tech companies (TCS, Amazon, Microsoft) lack a dedicated departmental bulletin board.
  • Informal Job Sharing: Opportunities posted in chat groups quickly get buried under casual chatter.
  • No Access Control: Inability to verify whether applicants meet department prerequisites.

4. Untracked Department Endowment Pledges

  • Opaque Department Initiatives: Alumni are unaware of departmental infrastructure needs (such as AI GPU clusters and student scholarship funds).
  • Lack of Real-Time Progress: No transparent tracking of funding targets versus actual contributions.
  • No Institutional Audit Trail: Accreditation bodies (NAAC/NBA) require verifiable records of alumni financial support.

Database Design & 3NF Schema Architecture

Normalized Relational Model Ensuring Data Integrity and Zero Redundancy
Database Design

Normalized Relational Schema (10 Tables)

MySQL 8.0
  • departments: dept_id (PK), dept_code (UQ), dept_name
  • verified_graduates: grad_id (PK), register_no (UQ), dept_id (FK), batch_year
  • users: user_id (PK), email (UQ), password_hash, salt, role
  • students: student_id (PK), user_id (FK), register_no (UQ), dept_id (FK)
  • alumni: alumni_id (PK), user_id (FK), register_no (UQ), company, designation
  • mentorship_sessions: session_id (PK), student_id (FK), alumni_id (FK), topic, status
  • jobs: job_id (PK), alumni_id (FK), job_title, company, salary_range
  • endowment_projects: project_id (PK), dept_id (FK), target_amount, current_amount
  • pledges: pledge_id (PK), project_id (FK), user_id (FK), pledge_amount
  • notifications: notification_id (PK), user_id (FK), title, message, is_read

3NF Normalization Principles Applied

Integrity

First Normal Form (1NF): All table columns contain atomic, indivisible values. No multi-valued repeating groups.

Second Normal Form (2NF): Every non-key attribute is fully functionally dependent on the entire primary key (surrogate auto-increment keys).

Third Normal Form (3NF): No transitive dependencies. Department names are isolated in departments and linked via dept_id foreign keys rather than redundantly duplicated.

Referential Actions: ON DELETE CASCADE for dependent profiles, jobs, sessions, and notifications. ON DELETE RESTRICT for academic departments.

Security & Transaction Engineering

Safeguards
  • 100% SQL Injection Prevention: Every query across all 9 DAOs binds user inputs via JDBC PreparedStatement placeholders (?). Zero raw SQL concatenation.
  • ACID Transaction Management: In EndowmentDAO.java, financial pledges execute under conn.setAutoCommit(false). The pledge insert and campaign total increment commit atomically; rolls back on error.
  • Salted Cryptographic Hashing: Passwords stored as SHA-256 hashes salted with unique random seeds per account.
  • Strict Server-Side RBAC: Even if client-side UI buttons are tampered with, Java controllers validate session role headers and reject unauthorized actions with HTTP 403.
